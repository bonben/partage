Feature Monograph · Representation Learning

We study representations that help intelligent systems learn, adapt, and make sense of complex data across signals, images, and brains.

From 60,000 hours of human cortical dynamics to low-rank tensor geometry and resource-frugal vision models: inside the mathematical foundations of adaptive intelligence.

BRAIN is a research team of Lab-STICC (UMR CNRS 6285) hosted at IMT Atlantique in Brest, France, within the Mathematical and Electrical Engineering (MEE) department. We investigate fundamental and applied questions at the intersection of Artificial Intelligence, Deep Learning, Signal Processing, Computer Vision, Audio, and Computational Neuroscience.

Neuroscience & EEG Foundation Models

REVE: A Foundation Model for EEG

By Yassine El Ouahidi, Jonathan Lys, Philipp Tholke, Nicolas Farrugia, Bastien Pasdeloup, Vincent Gripon, and Karim Jerbi

A 4D spatial-temporal foundation model pretrained on 60,000+ hours of EEG designed to adapt seamlessly to diverse clinical and cognitive tasks.

"By treating electroencephalography as a four-dimensional spatiotemporal manifold rather than a rigid channel matrix, REVE transfers across unseen sensor montages and clinical protocols."
60,000+ hrs Pretraining recordings across diverse cohorts
4D Encoding 3D spatial coordinates coupled with temporal streams
Universal Adapts to clinical & cognitive downstream benchmarks

TensLoRA: Tensor Alternatives for Low-Rank Adaptation

By Axel Marmoret, Reda Bensaid, Jonathan Lys, Vincent Gripon, and Francois Leduc-Primeau

Leveraging higher-order tensor factorizations across attention projections and layers to achieve superior parameter compression.

"Coupling attention projections and transformer depth into a unified higher-order tensor unlocks extreme parameter economy without sacrificing fine-tuning expressivity."
Higher-Order Tensor decomposition beyond 2D matrices
Cross-Layer Joint factorization of attention projections
Edge-Ready Minimal memory footprint for adaptation
Foundational Agenda

Three Scientific Challenges

SECTION 02 · RESEARCH PILLARS

Our research agenda is structured around three complementary questions on the nature of efficient representations.

Challenge I Low-Data Regime

Thrifty AI

How to find efficient representations in the low-data regime (few-shot learning, zero-shot adaptation, vision-language models, transfer learning, foundation model adaptation).

Key Focus Areas Few-shot learning · Zero-shot adaptation · Vision-language models · Transfer learning · Foundation model adaptation
Challenge II Frugal Compute & Energy

Compression of AI

How to find efficient representations to lower the computational and energy demands of AI systems (quantization, pruning, low-rank tensor factorization, zero-cost Neural Architecture Search, edge and embedded inference).

Key Focus Areas Quantization · Pruning · Low-rank tensor factorization · Zero-cost Neural Architecture Search · Edge & embedded inference
Challenge III Latent & Domain Structure

Geometry and AI

How to leverage the geometry of latent spaces or input domains to better adapt and monitor AI systems (graph signal processing, Graph Neural Networks, manifold learning, Riemannian and hyperbolic representations).

Key Focus Areas Graph signal processing · Graph Neural Networks · Manifold learning · Riemannian & hyperbolic representations

Eight Transversal Themes

SECTION 03 · THEMATIC SPECTRUM

Connecting theoretical machine learning with neuroimaging, acoustics, digital communications, and combinatorial optimization.

THEME 01

Few-shot & transfer

Rapid adaptation of pretrained representations and vision-language models under strict sample constraints.

THEME 02

Efficient & edge AI

Resource-conscious deep learning architectures designed for low-power hardware and embedded inference.

THEME 03

Graphs & geometry

Graph signal processing, Graph Neural Networks, and non-Euclidean latent spaces (Riemannian and hyperbolic).

THEME 04

Robustness

Monitoring, distribution shift resilience, and reliable generalization of deep neural representations.

THEME 05

Neuroscience & EEG

Foundation models for neuroimaging, brain-computer interfaces, and large-scale cortical dynamics.

THEME 06

Audio & music

Structural music analysis, bioacoustics, and self-supervised auditory representations.

THEME 07

Channel coding

Polar codes, error-correcting decoders, and high-throughput hardware transceivers.

THEME 08

Optimization

Combinatorial optimization and metaheuristics for complex discrete and continuous search spaces.

Selected Publications in Review

SECTION 04 · RECENT PAPERS

Complementing our feature spotlights on REVE and TensLoRA, these works examine kernel perspectives on vision-language adaptation and data-agnostic zero-cost proxies.

Few-shot & transfer Vision-Language

ProKeR: A Kernel Perspective on Few-Shot Adaptation of Large Vision-Language Models

Yassir Bendou, Amine Ouasfi, Vincent Gripon, Adnane Boukhayma

Reformulating few-shot adaptation of CLIP-like models through proximal kernel ridge regression in feature space.

Theoretical Insight · Thrifty AI Bridges classical kernel methods and modern foundation models, offering a closed-form, regularized alternative to heuristic prompt or adapter tuning when labeled examples are scarce.
Efficient & edge AI NAS

Insights From a Data- and Space-Agnostic Approach to Zero-Cost Proxies

Timotee Ly-Manson, Mathieu Leonardon, Abdeldjalil Aissa-El-Bey

Disentangling architectural topology from input data in zero-cost Neural Architecture Search proxies to enable fast hardware-aware design.

Architectural Insight · Compression of AI Reveals how structural network topology governs zero-cost scoring independently of input batches, accelerating hardware-constrained neural architecture search.
